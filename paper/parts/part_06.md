23. The position of Ficalhoa Hiern with respect to Sladenia
Kurz (Sladeniaceae) and Pentaphylacaceae was unresolved in Zuntini et al. (2024), but in Yu et al. (2025), the
former is well supported as sister to Pentaphylacaceae in
analyses of nuclear DNA data (but not in their plastid
trees). A merger of these two families under the older
name Pentaphylacaceae is thus appropriate, given the
lack of clear morphological apomorphies separating
Sladenia from Ficalhoa and other Pentaphylacaceae
(provided in Yu et al., 2025). Previously, Gilg &
Werdermann (1925) and Robson (1962) considered
Sladenieae and Ficalhoeae as tribes of Pentaphylacaceae, and Weitzman et al. (2004) also included them in
Pentaphylacaceae. These proposals are complemented
by the nuclear genomic analyses.
24. The families related to Orobanchaceae have experienced
an ever‐shifting number and generic composition since
this family was recircumscribed to include several former
genera of traditionally recognized Scrophulariaceae.
Several small new families forming a grade to crown
Orobanchaceae have been proposed, including Mazaceae, Paulowniaceae, Phrymaceae, Rehmanniaceae, and
Wightiaceae (Beardsley & Olmstead, 2002; Reveal, 2011, 2012; Liu et al., 2020; Xia et al., 2021). As
data became available for additional genera (Xia
et al., 2009), unexpected relationships have been
discovered, and new families have been proposed. For
example, Wightia Spreng. ex DC. had been included in
Paulowniaceae on morphological grounds until it was
sequenced and found not to be sister to Paulownia
Siebold & Zucc., resulting in a new family, Wightiaceae,
being described (Liu et al., 2020). Furthermore, Xia et al.
(2019) proposed that Wightia may be of hybrid origin
between Phrymaceae and Paulowniaceae, and should
thus be considered incertae sedis. Another genus
previously thought to be a member of Paulowniaceae,
Brandisia Hook.f. & Thomson, has now been shown to be
a member of Orobanchaceae (Xia et al., 2019). Material
of Shiuyinghua silvestrii (Pamp. & Bonati) Paclt
(described as a species of Catalpa Scop.) has not been
available for sequencing, so it remains with Paulownia,
where the specimen (in Florence, FI) ﬁts morphologically
(JW Byng, unpubl.). Given that morphology has failed to
facilitate an accurate assessment of relationships among
these genera, continuing instability of family circumscriptions is likely. Although the problems with placement and circumscription of Paulowniaceae present
intriguing evolutionary problems, they represent threats
to stability. Rehmanniaceae were already included in
Orobanchaceae (APG IV, 2016; Xia et al., 2021), and we
continue this trend and expand Orobanchaceae here to
include Mazaceae, Paulowniaceae, Phrymaceae, and
Wightiaceae, which will stabilize the circumscription of
Orobanchaceae while the process of establishing generic
phylogenetic relationships and erection of new subfamilies/tribes continues.

25. In APG IV (2016), Peltanthera Benth. was tentatively left
unplaced in the clade with Gesneriaceae and Calceolariaceae. However, immediately after publication of APG IV,
Molinari‐Novoa (2016) published Peltantheraceae, but not
based on any additional information or data. Peltanthera
has usually been associated with Gesneriaceae (Oxelman
et al., 1999; Clark et al., 2010), and Luna et al. (2019) found
Peltanthera closer to Gesneriaceae than Calceolariaceae,
but with poor support, unlike Refulio‐Rodríguez &
Olmstead (2014), who recovered Peltanthera as sister to
Gesneriaceae + Calceolariaceae. The broader clade here is
consistent in all recent phylogenetic studies, and therefore, the most stable solution is to unite all three into the
single family Gesneriaceae, perhaps with a subfamily
classiﬁcation that includes Calceolarioideae Luerss. and
Peltantheroideae Molinari in addition to Didymocarpoideae Arnott, Gesnerioideae Link, and Sanangoideae
A.Weber, J.L.Clark & Mich.Möller (Weber et al., 2013).
An enlarged Gesneriaceae are united by inﬂorescence
morphology, the ultimate units of the inﬂorescence being
pair‐ﬂowered cymes omnipresent in Calceolariaceae and
almost all Gesneriaceae (Weber, 2013). However, these
are not found in Peltanthera, which has axillary, manyﬂowered panicles, but not all Gesneriaceae have pairﬂowered cymes, exceptions being Sinningia Nees (singleﬂowered) and Loxonia Jack and Stauranthera Benth.
(single species of each having ordinary cymes;Weber, 2013), so inclusion of Peltanthera and Calceolariaceae, the latter with pair‐ﬂowered cymes, would not
add otherwise unrepresented morphological diversity to
Gesneriaceae.
26. The positions of Escalloniaceae and Paracryphiaceae
require more study. In Zuntini et al. (2024), Escalloniaceae formed a grade to Paracryphiaceae, but this was
weakly supported, and gene‐tree discordance exists at
these nodes. We continue to recognize Paracryphiales
and Escalloniales but note here that changes may be
needed in the future.
27. Oncothecaceae are well supported as an isolated
campanulid family (Stull et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2020)
excluded from the other orders, and thus, we recognize
Oncothecales Doweld.
28. Cassinopsis Sond. has been transferred from Icacinaceae to
Metteniusaceae, which illustrates plastid–nuclear discordance (Stull et al., 2020); nuclear analyses correlate well
with micromorphological synapomorphies. Cassinopsis was
morphologically out of place in Icacinaceae.
29. In the plastid results of Li et al. (2021) and others,
Cardiopteridales were included in Aquifoliales, but here,
they are accepted as a separate order because they are
sister to the larger lamiid clade (Zhang et al., 2020;
Zuntini et al., 2024). They include Stemonuraceae and
Cardiopteridaceae.
30. Expanding monogeneric Helwingiaceae to include
monogeneric Phyllonomaceae (both Aquifoliales in APG
IV, 2016) would then have epiphyllous inﬂorescences as a
potential synapomorphy. Merging this sister pair would
decrease redundancy in the system and has been
consistent with the plastid results of several studies
(Savolainen et al., 2000b; Li et al. 2021) and the more
recent nuclear results of Zuntini et al. (2024).

31. Columelliaceae (including Desfontainia Ruiz & Pav.) are
no longer part of Bruniales and are treated here as an
unplaced family close to Dipsacales and Escalloniales
(Zhang et al., 2020; Zuntini et al., 2024). If a position
sister to both these orders can be established for
Columelliaceae, then recognition of Columelliales Mart.
would be logical. The name Columelliales has priority
over Desfontainiales Takht., and although priority does
not apply above the rank of family, we would prefer to
use priority as the primary criterion in the case of
multiple, rarely used ordinal names.
32. Bruniales (without Columelliaceae) are unresolved in the
campanulids (Zuntini et al. (2024), along with Oncothecales and Metteniusales, and thus, they are no longer
close to Dipsacales/Apiales, as in plastid analyses
(Li et al., 2021).
33. In Dipsacales, Viburnaceae has been conserved over
Adoxaceae of APG II, III, and IV (Turland et al., 2018). If
Adoxa L. s.l. is included in this family, the name
Viburnaceae is now to be used.

7 Taxa incertae sedis
APG IV (2016) listed seven taxa as unplaced (incertae sedis).
These were:
1. Atrichodendron tonkinense Gagnep. was placed in
Solanaceae based on a poorly preserved specimen. It is
certainly not Solanaceae in morphological terms, but
characters are unclear in the single known specimen, and
it has never been sequenced. It therefore remains
unplaced to family.
2. Coptocheile macrorrhiza Hoﬀmanns. is a synonym of
Sinningia bulbosa (Ker.Gawl.) Wiehler (Gesneriaceae), a
species from southeastern Brazil (POWO, 2025).
3. Gumillea auriculata Ruiz & Pav. might belong to Picramniaceae, as suggested by Shipunov et al. (2020) and
Christenhusz et al. (2017). It was described and illustrated
from Peru, but no specimens are known. It is generally
similar in morphology to Picramnia, but the ﬂowers are
bisexual, and the illustration shows stipules, which
resemble pseudostipules of some species of Picramnia
and some Huerteales. However, the genus will remain
unplaced to family because no specimens are known.
4. Hirania rosea Thulin was conﬁrmed as Sapindaceae by
Buerki et al. (2021).
5. Keithia brasiliensis Spreng. remains unplaced. Suitable
material for DNA analysis remains to be found. It was
described as having a single sepal, ﬁve petals, and eight
stamens, which does not match characters needed for
inclusion in Capparaceae as originally suggested.
6. Poilanedora unijuga Gagnep. is similar to Keithia (above),
but there are known specimens. It has leaves with
two pinnae and ﬁve petals, like some Zygophyllaceae.
However, the herbarium specimens have not yet yielded
usable DNA data (F. Forest, pers. comm.), so its position
has yet to be conﬁrmed.
7. Rumphia amboinensis L. has been shown most likely to be
a synonym of Canarium strictum Roxb. (Burseraceae) by
